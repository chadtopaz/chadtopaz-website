Your EMT course: what to do, where, and how you know it's done

This guide is an overview of the information in your syllabus, schedule and onboarding emails.

What you will do

  • Learn the material online each week.
  • Practice hands-on skills in class and record every attempt.
  • Pass the exams along the way.
  • Care for real patients on supervised shifts.
  • Take the national EMT exam, then apply for your state license to work.

1. After you accept a seat

Start now, because some of this takes weeks. The links and forms are in your acceptance email.

Do this Done when
Pay the course fee. The payment website confirms that your payment went through
Take the FEMA courses and the Vermont Mandatory Reporter training. FEMA asks for a free student ID first. If you took a course before, your old certificate counts. You have each certificate saved as a file, ready to upload in section 2
Have a doctor, physician assistant or nurse fill in the program's two health forms: immunizations (vaccines) and a screening for fitting your N95 mask. An office printout is not accepted. Give the forms to your instructor. Your instructor confirms. You cannot start hospital shifts without them.

2. Before the first class

An email with your access codes arrives shortly before the first class. Set up these tools and check that each one opens.

Tool What it is Working when

EMT Ready (also called Limmer, LC-Ready or EMT Review Plus) Review questions, some assigned as homework You can log in and see the study resources
EMCE The app that records your attendance, skills and patients You have joined your cohort, your class group. Check it before you save: only EMCE's support team can fix a wrong one.
National Registry account Where you apply for and book the national exam You have created the account and completed the EMT application. Two programs have almost the same name, so pick the one your instructions name.
LIGHTS account Vermont's EMS licensing system: report enrollment now, apply for your license at the end You have submitted the Course Enrollment Notification with your class's course number, naming any Vermont EMS agency you belong to. A late form can hold up your licensing.

JB Learning has an assignment for each certificate from section 1, one for your National Registry account and one for your LIGHTS account. That list is the full one, so take any course on it that your acceptance email left out. Upload each certificate. For the National Registry and LIGHTS assignments, follow the steps written there, then type your name in the text box and submit. These may never be marked complete: once submitted, assume it is fine unless your instructor says otherwise.

Updates go to the email you applied with, so check it often. Then start section 3: the first unit's work comes before the first class.

3. Every week

The course is split into units, each on one topic, such as the airway. Every unit has online homework to finish before its class day. Your schedule lists each unit's chapters and exam days. Your instructor's email confirms the homework and the deadline.

Do this Done when
In JB Learning, read the chapters, then finish each one's Interactive Lecture and Assessment in Action. You can redo both. Each shows a grade in the Gradebook, which lists every graded item in the course. A dash means not done yet, except on the section 2 assignments and EMT Ready, which may stay a dash.
In EMT Ready, open the Review Menu and work through each topic you were assigned. EMT Ready tells you no items are left in that topic
Prepare for the next unit exam: a supervised test on recent units, taken in class on a testing site called FISDAP. You must pass every one. FISDAP shows your overall score as soon as you submit, and you can open it again there later.

Missing homework early in the course can put you on probation, a formal warning that can end in removal from the course. Your syllabus has that rule, the passing score and the retake rule.

4. Every class day

In class you practice hands-on skills. Each time you do one, someone watches you: an instructor or, for some skills, a classmate. EMCE calls that person your evaluator. You submit every attempt in EMCE, every time you do it. The syllabus labels the three kinds of practice P1, P2 and P3:

  • Skill (P1): one task on its own, such as suctioning an airway. Enter it as a Skill Eval.
  • Scenario (P2): a short made-up patient where you combine several skills. Enter it under Simulations as a Solo Sim Patient Eval, and add each skill you performed under Skills.
  • Simulation (P3): a full call with a team, from arriving to handing the patient over. Each student enters their own Solo Sim Patient Eval, with skills added the same way.
Do this Done when
Arrive on time and in uniform. In EMCE, find the class under Schedule and clock in. Clock out when you leave. Both are required. Both times show on that class in EMCE
After each attempt, submit it in EMCE and name your evaluator. Ask them to approve it before you leave. It has moved from Submitted, where it waits for approval, to Done

Your progress reports count each approved entry. To see which ones counted, open them on a tablet or laptop and tap your count. The EMCE guide has the steps.

Lateness lowers your attendance grade. If you cannot come, email your instructor before class. Uniform rules and the full list of what you must complete are in your syllabus.

5. On shift

Partway through the course you start real shifts under a preceptor: the experienced provider who supervises you and signs off your record. There are three kinds, and you record them in EMCE too:

  • Ride time: shifts with an ambulance crew. Three 12-hour shifts, 36 hours in all.
  • Clinical time: a shift in a hospital emergency department. One 12-hour shift.
  • Capstone: your final shifts, where you lead the care of some patients yourself. Two 12-hour shifts, 24 hours in all. Each patient you lead is called a patient lead.
Do this Done when
Wait for clearance: your instructor announces in class when you may start. Hours worked before that do not count. Shifts you can sign up for appear under Schedule in EMCE
Under Schedule, pick a shift and sign up. You are signed up for the shift
On the shift, clock in and open a shift record. Add each patient you helped care for, with no name or other identifying detail. Every patient you helped with is in the record
Before you leave, ask your preceptor to sign it off, then clock out. On capstone, ask them to confirm each patient you led. The shift is under Done. Its hours show in the Shift Attendance report and its patients in the Patient Evaluations Progress report. EMCE is the only record of your hours.

6. Finishing: the national exam and your license

Finish the course to take the national exam. Pass it to apply for a state license, your legal permission to work as an EMT.

Do this Done when
Wait for the program to tell the National Registry that you met the course requirements. Your application went in during section 2, so there is nothing to finish. If your exam fee is still unpaid, pay it now, unless you belong to a Vermont EMS agency: the state covers it. The National Registry emails you that you can schedule your exam. That permission is called an Authorization to Test, and it expires.
Schedule the exam from your National Registry account, and take it. Your result shows in your account
Apply for your Vermont license in LIGHTS. The license is issued
